# History: the dog-show sites and the AS/400

## The machines

The **IBM AS/400** (Application System/400) launched in June 1988 — a *midrange*
computer, not a mainframe (a distinction its defenders still litigate).
It descended from the System/38 (1979), which came from the System/3 line (1969).
IBM discontinued the AS/400 branding in 2000 (became iSeries), but the platform
lives on as IBM i.

The early boxes ran on an unusual CISC architecture with an abstracted
instruction set — the OS (OS/400) sits so deep in the machine that the line
between code and microcode is tenuous. Programs were largely written in RPG.

## The companies

- **Jack Onofrio Dog Shows, LLC** (Oklahoma City, OK) — AKC-licensed show
  superintendent. Their entry-processing system was originally built on/for an
  IBM AS/400 in the 1990s, and the website is still managed by the engineer who
  built it.
- **MB-F, Inc.** (Greensboro, NC) — the other great superintendent, behind
  infodog.com. Their president built the InfoDog site himself.
- There is a friendly tribal divide in the dog fancy over which is better.
  InfoDog's site is routinely mocked for being unintuitive; Onofrio's is loved
  (at least by this researcher) precisely for wearing its age openly.

## The 1997 forum post

The Onofrio engineer posted asking for AS/400 help on an AS/400 forum around
1997, mentioning onofrio.com. The post is believed to live in the Usenet
archive for `comp.sys.ibm.as400.misc` (Google Groups rate-limited the search;
retrieval pending). Taylor's original research on this is in her ChatGPT
history.
